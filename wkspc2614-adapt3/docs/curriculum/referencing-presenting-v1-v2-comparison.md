## Referencing — Advanced

### Course outcomes

| # | Original | v2 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Articulate what academic integrity means and the values that underpin it. | Justify a course of action when a weak or disappointing result raises an ethical choice between honest reporting and presenting a stronger impression. |
| 2 | Justify why referencing matters as scholarly practice, beyond avoiding plagiarism. | Justify acting to correct an unsupported figure or claim in shared work, upholding honesty even where the error would otherwise go unnoticed. |

| 6 | — | Recognise and act on an ethical responsibility in a new professional role without being asked, and explain the habit to someone newer than yourself. |

Note the subject-matter shift at advanced: the **original** advanced tier
stays on referencing-as-scholarly-practice (integrity's values, why
referencing matters, quote/paraphrase judgement, synthesis). The **v2**
advanced tier moves to broader **research-ethics judgement scenarios** (a
weak result, an unnoticed error, a fairness issue, an unacknowledged
contributor, a profession's expectations, an unprompted responsibility) -
referencing mechanics are no longer the subject; ethical judgement is.
