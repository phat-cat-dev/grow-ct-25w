## Part 0 Summary

### 0.1 Agent definition
<!-- brief: Two or three sentences for an agent: what this is and when to reach for it, and one thing it is not for. Write last, from the finished document. -->
<!-- label: editorial; status: drafted; basis: secondary -->
Creativity is one of thirteen academic skills pathways: creativity as a developable academic skill in every discipline, from reframing it as a skill rather than a gift (divergent and convergent thinking, its partnership with critical thinking, overcoming fear of failure and perfectionism), through creative methods in study (problem framing and reframing, generating several solutions, combinatorial creativity and the adjacent possible, cognitive flexibility), to creativity as innovation, enterprise and a professional attribute (the enterprise pipeline of awareness, mindset, capability and effectiveness). Reach for it to place, sequence or assess a capability in creative thinking or enterprise in study. It is not about evaluating ideas, which is Criticality's, nor about the arts as a subject.

### 0.2 Summary
<!-- brief: One paragraph: who proposed it, when, what it is, its main use and its main dispute. Write last. -->
<!-- label: both; status: drafted; basis: secondary -->
The academic skills pathways model (2026) sets Creativity as one of thirteen pathways, authored from an institutional library guide and the QAA's enterprise and entrepreneurship guidance rather than drawn from the study skills guide, which uses creativity only as the topic of a worked research example. Its framing moves creativity away from the arts and enterprise towards a foundational academic skill across all disciplines; enterprise is one downstream application, entering at Advanced. It climbs in three tiers: creativity as a foundational skill; creative methods in academic work; creativity as innovation, enterprise and professional attribute. The model asks for subject expert sign-off before authoring and notes that the enterprise strand could spin out later. Its open questions are which theory to make key and how far the institutional guide's claims are evidenced (Part E).

- Three tiers, Introductory, Intermediate and Advanced, each with a focus, content and a goal (D:PTM, Pathway 13).
- An authored pathway: content is authored from institutional and sector sources, recorded for sign-off; the mapping needs subject expert approval before authoring (D:PTM, Pathway 13).

### A2 Core principles
<!-- brief: One item per claim the theory rests on, each cited to the authors. Claims only, not uses or benefits. -->
<!-- label: source; status: drafted; basis: primary -->
- Creativity is not an innate artistic gift possessed by a few but a developable skill that matters in every discipline (D:PTM, Pathway 13, Introductory; D:CMETCREATIVE, by summary).
- In academic contexts creativity is generating novel ideas, making unexpected connections, approaching problems from several angles and synthesising information in new ways (D:CMETCREATIVE, by summary).
- Creativity and critical thinking work together: creativity generates ideas, critical thinking evaluates them (D:PTM, Pathway 13; D:CMETCREATIVE, by summary).
- Barriers such as fear of failure and perfectionism can be recognised and overcome, allowing "bad" ideas alongside good ones (D:PTM, Pathway 13, Introductory).
- Creative problem-solving frames and reframes a problem and generates several solutions before settling (D:PTM, Pathway 13, Intermediate).
- New ideas often come from combining existing elements (combinatorial creativity) at the edges of what exists (the adjacent possible) and across disciplines (D:PTM, Pathway 13, Intermediate; D:CMETCREATIVE, by summary).
- Cognitive flexibility, adapting one's approach rather than holding to one strategy, is part of creativity in learning (D:PTM, Pathway 13, Intermediate; D:CMETCREATIVE, by summary).
- Creativity is a professional, value-creating capability; enterprise education progresses from awareness through mindset and capability to effectiveness, and is taught about, for and through enterprise (D:PTM, Pathway 13, Advanced, after D:QAAEE).
- Creativity features prominently among graduate attributes, in the sense of thinking innovatively to solve problems (D:EAU, p. 14).

### A4 Concepts used
<!-- brief: The ideas the theory rests on that other theories share; each will become or match a TERMINOLOGY-DAT concept. -->
<!-- label: both; status: drafted; basis: primary -->
- creativity: in academic work, generating novel ideas, making unexpected connections, approaching problems from several angles and synthesising in new ways (D:CMETCREATIVE, by summary).
- divergent-thinking: generating many possible ideas or solutions (D:PTM, Pathway 13; after D:GUILFORD).
- convergent-thinking: narrowing to the best idea or solution (D:PTM, Pathway 13; after D:GUILFORD).
- creative-problem-solving: framing and reframing a problem and generating several solutions before settling (D:PTM, Pathway 13).
- combinatorial-creativity: innovation by combining existing elements in unexpected ways (D:CMETCREATIVE, by summary).
- adjacent-possible: innovation at the edges of what already exists (D:PTM, Pathway 13, after D:JOHNSON).
- lateral-thinking: approaching a problem indirectly to escape habitual patterns (D:CMETCREATIVE, after D:DEBONO).
- cognitive-flexibility: the capacity to change approach rather than hold rigidly to one strategy (D:CMETCREATIVE, by summary).
- enterprise-education: education for awareness, mindset, capability and effectiveness in creating value (D:PTM, Pathway 13, after D:QAAEE).
- critical-thinking: evaluating ideas; creativity's partner (shared with `path:criticality`).

### A5a How practitioners apply it
<!-- brief: From the practitioner's side: the steps they take, the tools and artefacts they use, a worked example, and the decisions they make along the way. Separate what the authors prescribe (source) from how it is done in practice (reported, with sources) and what follows from it (editorial). -->
<!-- label: both; status: drafted; basis: primary -->
Practitioners here are tutors, academic skills staff, librarians, enterprise educators and authors of learning material.

**What the authors prescribe** (source):
- Apply creative techniques to real study tasks: brainstorming, mind mapping, free writing, creative note-taking and reading, creative approaches to essays and revision (D:PTM, Pathway 13, Intermediate).
- Use discipline-specific examples from the institutional guide's sub-pages (D:PTM, Pathway 13, notes; D:CMETCREATIVE).
- Teach enterprise about, for and through practice (D:PTM, Pathway 13, Advanced, after D:QAAEE).

**How it is done in practice** (reported):
- University libraries publish creative thinking guides with discipline pages (D:CMETCREATIVE; evidence: reported).
- Tutors set innovative assessment tasks beyond traditional genres (D:EAU, p. 65; evidence: reported).

**Worked example**:
At Introductory, the learner generates many angles on an essay question without judging, then chooses one with critical thinking. At Intermediate, they reframe a stuck problem from another discipline and justify the approach with combinatorial creativity. At Advanced, they develop an idea into a proposal for a partner, testing its value and tolerating uncertainty (editorial).

### A5d Misapplications
<!-- brief: Common ways it is misused or misread, the evidence for each, and how to avoid it. -->
<!-- label: both; status: drafted; basis: primary -->

- Judging ideas while generating them (D:PTM, Pathway 13, Introductory; evidence: claimed); separate divergent and convergent stages.

### A8 Selection
<!-- brief: When to choose it over named alternatives, and what it suits badly. Our reading (editorial). -->
<!-- label: editorial; status: drafted; basis: secondary -->
Choose this pathway when the aim is generating ideas, solving problems in new ways or turning ideas into value. For evaluating ideas, choose `path:criticality`; for reflecting on one's own creative process, `path:reflective-practice`; for enterprise in placements, `path:work-based-experiential-learning`. It suits badly a need for subject-specific artistic technique.
