# Reflective Practice: the cards in context

D-ing-0055 keeps each card in the core as the pathway template defines it and puts here what lies beyond the template: each card's vocabulary, the book topics under it, the benchmark evidence, the crosswalk to other frameworks, the pedagogy beneath it and how other authors describe it. This file speaks to the core; it is not part of it. It is the pilot: its shape is the golden template the other twelve pathways follow (D-ing-0061).

Sources: the cards and their theory and frameworks from the core's A10; vocabulary from `ing-card-vocabulary.json` 0.1.0; book topics from adp-0212's draft book map (`mta-nts-ho-adp-nts-0007`), every quote and page re-checked against the guide's Markdown copy today; benchmark evidence from adp-0212's card drafts (counts are passages / texts, of 54); other authors from META's INGEST runs in UNI's `library/ingested/theory/`, each with its SHA-256 prefix, paraphrased from the run's agent definition, never quoted. Quotations from the guide stay within 25 words.

How the benchmark evidence is read: **skill** where the passage describes the learner doing the capability; **theme** where reflective practice appears only as an item in a list of attributes, methods or assessment types, the cross-cutting language the tagging review is to separate.

## 3a. Tier statements and the kinds of knowledge

A loosely level-based set of statements about the practice at each tier, read through Anderson and Krathwohl's knowledge dimension (`ing:doc:bloom-anderson-krathwohl-2001`, 738db9b93165): factual knowledge (the terms and details), conceptual knowledge (how they fit together: models, principles), procedural knowledge (how to do it, and the criteria for choosing which method) and metacognitive knowledge (knowledge of cognition and of one's own), on a continuum from concrete to abstract. Factual and conceptual together are what Biggs and Marzano call declarative knowledge, knowing that; Biggs calls knowledge put to work functioning knowledge (`ing:doc:constructive-alignment-biggs-1996`); Marzano and Kendall place metacognition and the self-system above the cognitive system (`ing:doc:new-taxonomy-marzano-kendall-2007`, 988e7f88bf23). The statements are ing's, drawn from the model's tier text and the cards; the classification is ing's reading.

| Tier | Statement about the practice | Knowledge type | Cognitive process | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Introductory | Knows the words: reflection, the three stages (describe, reflect, take action), action, reflective journal. | factual | remember | |
| Introductory | Explains why reflection is asked for and what each stage is for. | conceptual | understand | |
| Introductory | Applies the three-stage cycle to one learning experience, positives before negatives. | procedural (one method) | apply | declarative knowledge first put to work, with a set method |
| Introductory | Notices what went well and what did not in their own work. | metacognitive (beginning) | understand | self-knowledge appears, prompted |
| Intermediate | Tells critical reflection from description, and the depth a piece of reflection reaches. | conceptual | analyse | the concept of depth enters |
| Intermediate | Turns reflection into a dated action plan and uses a recognised model such as Gibbs. | procedural | apply | from one method to recognised models |
| Intermediate | Reflects on motivation and amends strategies that are not working. | metacognitive (strategic) | evaluate | knowledge of one's own strategies, used to change them |
| Intermediate | Writes reflection in a report for a professional reader. | procedural, conceptual | create | the genre of the field |
| Advanced | Chooses and justifies the model that fits the purpose (Gibbs, Kolb, Johns). | procedural (criteria for choosing) | evaluate | knowing when to use which method: the top of procedural knowledge |
| Advanced | Examines how own identity, values, assumptions and social position shape what they notice (reflexivity). | metacognitive (self-knowledge) | analyse, evaluate | knowledge of the self as knower |
| Advanced | Uses reflection as continuing professional development, against a profession's standards. | metacognitive with procedural | apply, create | functioning knowledge: the practice owned and used without prompting |

**How the learner moves to mastery.** Across the tiers the weight shifts along Anderson and Krathwohl's continuum, from concrete to abstract: Introductory is mostly declarative (factual and conceptual) with one procedure applied under guidance; Intermediate is mostly procedural, with metacognitive knowledge of one's own strategies beginning; Advanced is mostly metacognitive, with procedural knowledge at its highest form, the criteria for choosing a method. In Biggs's terms the movement is from declarative to functioning knowledge; in Marzano and Kendall's, from the cognitive system to metacognition and then the self-system, where the learner's own goals and identity direct the practice. Mastery is not knowing more about reflection but reflection becoming the learner's own way of working, which is the core's A11 ladder from compliance to flow.

**The map's text (Pathway 10, Reflective Practice)**

> **Source:** the reflection strand of Chapter 4, plus reflective models used across the book. Discrete. This is reflection as a *professional discipline* — formal, assessed, and career-long — distinct from the self-regulation mechanics folded into Pathway 2.
>
> **Introductory — what reflection is and why it matters.**
> Reflection as both a learner's tool and a professional practice; the simple ERA cycle (describe → reflect → take action); reflecting honestly (positives before negatives; no reflection is wholly positive or negative).
>
> **Intermediate — structured reflection turned into action.**
> Reflecting on real experiences (feedback, tasks, group work); avoiding pure description — reflecting *critically* on your own performance; turning reflection into concrete action plans.
>
> **Advanced — reflection as professional practice.**
> The fuller models (Gibbs, Kolb) used in assessed reflection in vocational fields (nursing, teaching, social work); reflection as continuing professional development; reflection as part of graduate identity.
>
> **Progression logic:** what reflection is → structured, critical reflection → professional reflective practice.
>
> **Cross-links:** the engine beneath the feedback cycle and self-assessment (Pathway 2), reflective writing (Pathway 6), the group-work reflective element (Pathway 7), and experiential learning (Pathway 11).
