# Starting, Progressing and Succeeding: the cards in context

D-ing-0055 keeps each card in the core as the pathway template defines it and puts here what lies beyond the template: each card's vocabulary, the book topics under it, the benchmark evidence, the crosswalk to other frameworks, the pedagogy beneath it and how other authors describe it, then the vocabulary across the cards, the tiers from two directions and a table of correspondences. This file speaks to the core; it is not part of it. Its shape is the golden template (`ing-cards-template.md`, D-ing-0061).

Sources: the cards and their theory and frameworks from the core's A10; vocabulary from `ing-card-vocabulary.json`; book topics from adp-0212's draft book map (`mta-nts-ho-adp-nts-0007`), every quote found in the guide's Markdown copy by script and its page recomputed from the copy's page markers; benchmark evidence from adp-0212's card drafts (counts are passages / texts, of 54), each extract read by ing; other authors from META's INGEST runs in UNI's `library/ingested/theory/`, each with its SHA-256 prefix, given from the run's agent definition, never quoting the author. ## 3a. Tier statements and the kinds of knowledge

A loosely level-based set of statements about the practice at each tier, read through Anderson and Krathwohl's knowledge dimension (`ing:doc:bloom-anderson-krathwohl-2001`, 738db9b93165): factual knowledge (the terms and details), conceptual knowledge (how they fit together: models, principles), procedural knowledge (how to do it, and the criteria for choosing which method) and metacognitive knowledge (knowledge of cognition and of one's own), on a continuum from concrete to abstract. Factual and conceptual together are what Biggs and Marzano call declarative knowledge, knowing that; Biggs calls knowledge put to work functioning knowledge (`ing:doc:constructive-alignment-biggs-1996`); Marzano and Kendall place metacognition and the self-system above the cognitive system (`ing:doc:new-taxonomy-marzano-kendall-2007`, 988e7f88bf23). The statements are ing's, built up from the cards and the knowledge models; the classification is ing's reading. Section 4a gives the pathway model's own tier text, from the other direction (D-ing-0059).

**How the learner moves to mastery.** This pathway lives in Marzano and Kendall's self-system more than any other: beliefs about one's own ability, motivation and emotional response. Across the tiers the weight moves from declarative knowledge of mindset and motivation, with a first recognition of one's own (Introductory), through procedures for sustaining goals and managing oneself under pressure (Intermediate), to self-direction and identity (Advanced). In Anderson and Krathwohl's terms it is metacognitive almost from the start, and the movement is from knowledge about oneself to acting on it unprompted. Mastery is becoming a graduate, the map's Advanced goal, which is the core's A11 ladder from compliance to flow.

**The map's text (Pathway 1, Starting, Progressing & Succeeding)**

> **Source:** Chapters 2 and 14. A full tiered pathway **and the programme's entry point** — surfaced first, it introduces the others and carries the orientation tone. This is the *dispositional* layer: who you're becoming and why you're motivated, distinct from the mechanics of learning (Pathway 2).
>
> **Introductory — Starting.**
> Finding your feet. What "excelling" actually means; the shift from school/college to university and why it feels different; growth versus fixed mindset; understanding your own motivation (mastery versus performance); setting goals and breaking them into achievable sub-goals; building self-efficacy (belief in your own capability).
>
> **Intermediate — Progressing.**
> Keeping going. Sustaining motivation across a whole course; handling setbacks, disappointment, and anxiety constructively; setting realistic expectations without tipping into paralysing perfectionism; knowing when and how to seek support — early rather than late.
>
> **Advanced — Succeeding.**
> Becoming a graduate. Independence and self-direction; consolidating a graduate identity; the transition out of university into professional life; becoming a lifelong learner who carries these dispositions into work.
>
> **Progression logic:** find your feet → sustain and adapt → become a graduate.
> **Cross-links:** every other pathway leans on this for the motivational/dispositional layer; Pathway 2 (self-regulated learning) is its mechanical counterpart.

The map's voice-band anchor governs the lesson register and is left out here.

**Where the two directions differ** (ing's reading, recorded, not resolved): the two agree on the order: finding one's feet (excelling, mindset, motivation, goals) at Introductory, keeping going (setbacks, anxiety, support) at Intermediate, becoming a graduate (independence, identity, transition, lifelong learning) at Advanced. The map places building self-efficacy at Introductory; d2 places it at Intermediate with managing anxiety, as the core's former 02 had it. Seeking support early, in the map's Intermediate text, has no card of its own.
