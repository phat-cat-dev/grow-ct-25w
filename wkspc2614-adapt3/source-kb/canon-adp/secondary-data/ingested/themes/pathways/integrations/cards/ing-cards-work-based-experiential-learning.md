# Work-Based and Experiential Learning: the cards in context

D-ing-0055 keeps each card in the core as the pathway template defines it and puts here what lies beyond the template: each card's vocabulary, the book topics under it, the benchmark evidence, the crosswalk to other frameworks, the pedagogy beneath it and how other authors describe it, then the vocabulary across the cards, the tiers from two directions and a table of correspondences. This file speaks to the core; it is not part of it. Its shape is the golden template (`ing-cards-template.md`, D-ing-0061).

Sources: the cards and their theory and frameworks from the core's A10; vocabulary from `ing-card-vocabulary.json`; book topics from adp-0212's draft book map (`mta-nts-ho-adp-nts-0007`), every quote found in the guide's Markdown copy by script and its page recomputed from the copy's page markers; benchmark evidence from adp-0212's card drafts (counts are passages / texts, of 54), each extract read by ing; other authors from META's INGEST runs in UNI's `library/ingested/theory/`, each with its SHA-256 prefix, given from the run's agent definition, never quoting the author. ## 3a. Tier statements and the kinds of knowledge

A loosely level-based set of statements about the practice at each tier, read through Anderson and Krathwohl's knowledge dimension (`ing:doc:bloom-anderson-krathwohl-2001`, 738db9b93165): factual knowledge (the terms and details), conceptual knowledge (how they fit together: models, principles), procedural knowledge (how to do it, and the criteria for choosing which method) and metacognitive knowledge (knowledge of cognition and of one's own), on a continuum from concrete to abstract. Factual and conceptual together are what Biggs and Marzano call declarative knowledge, knowing that; Biggs calls knowledge put to work functioning knowledge (`ing:doc:constructive-alignment-biggs-1996`); Marzano and Kendall place metacognition and the self-system above the cognitive system (`ing:doc:new-taxonomy-marzano-kendall-2007`, 988e7f88bf23). The statements are ing's, built up from the cards and the knowledge models; the classification is ing's reading. Section 4a gives the pathway model's own tier text, from the other direction (D-ing-0059).

| Introductory | Explains what learning in practice offers and how it applies classroom theory. | conceptual | understand |  |
| Introductory | Sets out what they want to learn from a placement. | metacognitive (beginning) | apply | one's own learning goals appear |

**How the learner moves to mastery.** Across the tiers the weight moves along Anderson and Krathwohl's continuum: Introductory is declarative, the kinds and value of learning in practice, with one's own goals set; Intermediate is procedural, conduct and the mentor, with theory tested against practice; Advanced is procedural at its highest, a model and a judgement, and metacognitive in the practitioner's reflection on their own practice and identity. In Lave and Wenger's terms the learner moves from legitimate peripheral participation towards full membership, and changes who they are as they do. Mastery is learning from experience to a professional standard, the map's Advanced goal, which is the core's A11 ladder from compliance to flow.
