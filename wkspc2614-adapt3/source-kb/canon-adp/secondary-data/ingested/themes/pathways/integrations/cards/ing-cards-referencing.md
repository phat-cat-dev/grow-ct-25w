## 3a. Tier statements and the kinds of knowledge

A loosely level-based set of statements about the practice at each tier, read through Anderson and Krathwohl's knowledge dimension (`ing:doc:bloom-anderson-krathwohl-2001`, 738db9b93165): factual knowledge (the terms and details), conceptual knowledge (how they fit together: models, principles), procedural knowledge (how to do it, and the criteria for choosing which method) and metacognitive knowledge (knowledge of cognition and of one's own), on a continuum from concrete to abstract. Factual and conceptual together are what Biggs and Marzano call declarative knowledge, knowing that; Biggs calls knowledge put to work functioning knowledge (`ing:doc:constructive-alignment-biggs-1996`); Marzano and Kendall place metacognition and the self-system above the cognitive system (`ing:doc:new-taxonomy-marzano-kendall-2007`, 988e7f88bf23). The statements are ing's, built up from the cards and the knowledge models; the classification is ing's reading. Section 4a gives the pathway model's own tier text, from the other direction (D-ing-0059).

**How the learner moves to mastery.** Across the tiers the weight moves along Anderson and Krathwohl's continuum, though not evenly: Introductory is declarative and procedural, what referencing is and how to do it, with keeping one's own records as the first metacognitive habit; Intermediate is procedural and strategic, quotation and paraphrase and citation used to show what one has done; Advanced returns to the conceptual, the values, and ends in the self-system, integrity as identity. The map builds from the concrete skill to the culminating "why", reversing the guide's order; in Marzano and Kendall's terms the learner moves from the cognitive system to the self-system, where values and identity direct conduct. Mastery is integrity as scholarly and professional practice, the map's Advanced goal, which is the core's A11 ladder from compliance to flow.

**The map's text (Pathway 4, Referencing, Citation & Academic Integrity)**

> **Source:** Chapter 10. Grouped, and taught **in that order**: referencing → citation → integrity.
>
> **Introductory — referencing.**
> The concrete, doable skill first. Why we reference; what references look like (in-text reference versus references list); the mechanics of author-date/Harvard for common source types (books, journal articles, web pages); accuracy and consistency (and why copy-pasting references introduces errors). *Basic plagiarism awareness* threads here too — enough to stay safe — even though the full values framework sits at advanced. Goal: reference correctly.
>
> **Intermediate — citation.**
> Citation as a purposeful tool, not a compliance chore. In-text citation as evidence — of the *scope* of your research (the references list is what many tutors read first), of critical engagement (references signpost where you're using sources to build an argument), and of disciplinary knowledge. Quotation versus paraphrase, and why paraphrasing is the more skilful and valuable act. Handling trickier sources. Goal: cite purposefully and paraphrase well.
>
> **Advanced — academic integrity.**
> The values framework underneath everything: the six ICAI values (honesty, trust, fairness, respect, responsibility, courage) and what each means in practice; plagiarism (intentional and unintentional) and how efficient working prevents it; integrity as a component of graduate identity carried into professional life. Goal: integrity as scholarly and professional practice.
>
> **Note on ordering (principles over literalism):** Chapter 10 opens with the integrity *values* then covers referencing. This pathway reverses that — start with the concrete skill (referencing), build to the ethical framework (integrity) as the culminating "why."
> **Cross-links:** referencing supports Academic Writing; research ethics-in-practice (consent, data handling) is shared with Researching (Pathway 9) — integrity covers the values and plagiarism, Researching covers ethics in the field; integrity connects to the graduate-identity thread in Pathway 1.

The map's voice-band anchor governs the lesson register and is left out here.

**Where the two directions differ** (ing's reading, recorded, not resolved): the two agree: referencing at Introductory, citation with quotation, paraphrase and trickier sources at Intermediate, the values, plagiarism and graduate identity at Advanced. The map threads basic plagiarism awareness through Introductory; here it is in i1's explanation and i3's records, with d1 carrying quotation and paraphrase.
