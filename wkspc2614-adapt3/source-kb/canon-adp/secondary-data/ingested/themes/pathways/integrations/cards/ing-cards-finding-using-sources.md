## 3a. Tier statements and the kinds of knowledge

A loosely level-based set of statements about the practice at each tier, read through Anderson and Krathwohl's knowledge dimension (`ing:doc:bloom-anderson-krathwohl-2001`, 738db9b93165): factual knowledge (the terms and details), conceptual knowledge (how they fit together: models, principles), procedural knowledge (how to do it, and the criteria for choosing which method) and metacognitive knowledge (knowledge of cognition and of one's own), on a continuum from concrete to abstract. Factual and conceptual together are what Biggs and Marzano call declarative knowledge, knowing that; Biggs calls knowledge put to work functioning knowledge (`ing:doc:constructive-alignment-biggs-1996`); Marzano and Kendall place metacognition and the self-system above the cognitive system (`ing:doc:new-taxonomy-marzano-kendall-2007`, 988e7f88bf23). The statements are ing's, built up from the cards and the knowledge models; the classification is ing's reading. Section 4a gives the pathway model's own tier text, from the other direction (D-ing-0059).

**How the learner moves to mastery.** Across the tiers the weight moves along Anderson and Krathwohl's continuum: Introductory is mostly declarative, what academic searching is and where to start, with one procedure (the discovery service) and a first look at what one already knows; Intermediate is procedural, the craft of keywords, operators and limiters, with monitoring of one's own search beginning; Advanced is procedural and metacognitive, extending and judging, and deciding for oneself what a comprehensive search needs. In Kuhlthau's terms the learner moves from the uncertainty of initiation to the confidence of a formed focus; in Willison and O'Regan's, from prescribed to open research. Mastery is the guide's "science" of searching become the learner's own, which is the core's A11 ladder from compliance to flow.

**The map's text (Pathway 3, Finding & Using Sources)**

> **Source:** Chapter 7. Discrete. This is searching and evaluating *sources* — distinct from conceiving a research *project* (Pathway 9).
>
> **Introductory — searching as a discipline.**
> The reframe first: academic searching is not looking up trivia — it's exploratory, iterative, and effortful, "a science." Why research matters; the library discovery service as first port of call; using the module reading list; the five-stage shape (plan → keywords → discovery service → evaluate → extend). The Natalie-versus-Jess search contrast anchors disorganised versus planned searching. Goal: reliably find sources using the discovery service.
>
> **Intermediate — search craft.**
> Planning by first reflecting on what you already know, to target gaps; identifying keywords and synonyms; Boolean operators (AND / OR / NOT / " " / ( ) / \*); limiters (date, subject, source type, availability); iterative cycles of narrowing. Goal: targeted, efficient searches that return the *right* results.
>
> **Advanced — extending and evaluating deeply.**
> Evaluating sources with discernment: source-type expectations; currency, authority, and — most importantly — relevance to *your own* knowledge-building. Extending beyond the discovery service: subject databases, citation searching, Google Scholar and Books, and the comprehensive searching a dissertation needs. The book frames "extending your search" as a final-year/dissertation activity, so this is a natural advanced tier. Goal: comprehensive, discerning engagement with the discourse.
>
> **Progression logic:** find sources → find the *right* sources efficiently → engage comprehensively and critically.
> **Cross-links:** feeds Criticality (evaluating sources), Academic Writing (integrating them), and Researching (the search phase of a project).

The map's voice-band anchor governs the lesson register and is left out here.

**Where the two directions differ** (ing's reading, recorded, not resolved): the map puts planning from what one already knows at Intermediate; i3 places it at Introductory (the former c1, ruled there). Otherwise the two agree: the reframe and the discovery service at Introductory; keywords, synonyms, Boolean operators, limiters and iterative narrowing at Intermediate; evaluation with discernment and extending the search at Advanced.
