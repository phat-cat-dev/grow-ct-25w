## 3a. Tier statements and the kinds of knowledge

A loosely level-based set of statements about the practice at each tier, read through Anderson and Krathwohl's knowledge dimension (`ing:doc:bloom-anderson-krathwohl-2001`, 738db9b93165): factual knowledge (the terms and details), conceptual knowledge (how they fit together: models, principles), procedural knowledge (how to do it, and the criteria for choosing which method) and metacognitive knowledge (knowledge of cognition and of one's own), on a continuum from concrete to abstract. Factual and conceptual together are what Biggs and Marzano call declarative knowledge, knowing that; Biggs calls knowledge put to work functioning knowledge (`ing:doc:constructive-alignment-biggs-1996`); Marzano and Kendall place metacognition and the self-system above the cognitive system (`ing:doc:new-taxonomy-marzano-kendall-2007`, 988e7f88bf23). The statements are ing's, built up from the cards and the knowledge models; the classification is ing's reading. Section 4a gives the pathway model's own tier text, from the other direction (D-ing-0059).

**How the learner moves to mastery.** Across the tiers the weight moves along Anderson and Krathwohl's continuum: Introductory is mostly declarative, what a research project is and what a question needs, with a first judgement of what one can research; Intermediate is procedural, the genres of the proposal, the review and the report, with one's own work placed in the field; Advanced is procedural at its highest form, choosing a methodology and applying ethics, and metacognitive in running a whole project. In Willison and O'Regan's terms the learner moves from prescribed towards open research, setting their own scope. The tier map notes that this pathway skews later than most, so its Introductory learner is already capable; mastery is independent research to a scholarly standard, which is the core's A11 ladder from compliance to flow.

**The map's text (Pathway 9, Researching & Research Methods)**

> **Source:** the research material the book scatters — the research proposal, literature review, and report (Chapter 9); research ethics and data handling (Chapter 10); the dissertation as the culminating integration of every literacy (Chapter 5); and the discourse-community framing (Chapter 3). This pathway pulls those pieces into one coherent progression. It is where the dissertation lives. **New in v2.**
>
> **Introductory — what research is and how a project is shaped.**
> Research as a structured project distinct from an essay; moving from a broad topic to a specific research question (the general-to-specific table in Chapter 9); the rationale ("why should this research exist?"); aims and objectives. Goal: frame a researchable question.
>
> **Intermediate — the research-document genres.**
> The research proposal (the full question set — title, rationale, aims, methodology, organisation, indicative bibliography — and the ultranationalism exemplar); the literature review as *critique* (mapping the discourse and synthesising, not describing); the report structure (sciences versus business variants). Goal: produce the documents that scaffold a research project.
>
> **Advanced — running research with rigour and ethics.**
> Methodology and approach (data, analysis, theoretical lenses); research ethics and the handling of data (the ESRC principles, consent, anonymisation, the ethics form) — drawn from Chapter 10; the dissertation as the culminating integration of every literacy. Goal: conduct independent research to a professional/scholarly standard.
>
> **Progression logic:** frame a question → produce the research documents → run research with rigour and ethics.
> **Notes:** this pathway is heavily final-year/postgraduate, so its tiers skew later than most — "introductory" here is already a fairly capable student, which is fine under a proficiency model. Research ethics is *shared* with Referencing/Integrity (Pathway 4): integrity covers values and plagiarism, Researching covers ethics-in-practice (consent, data) — they cross-reference rather than duplicate.
> **Cross-links:** builds on Finding & Using Sources (the search phase), Criticality (the literature review), and Academic Writing (clear expression), which now hands the research genres to this pathway.

The map's voice-band anchor governs the lesson register and is left out here.

**Where the two directions differ** (ing's reading, recorded, not resolved): the two agree: question, rationale and aims at Introductory; the proposal, the literature review as critique and the report at Intermediate; methodology, ethics and the dissertation at Advanced. The map notes that the whole pathway skews later; the indicative levels here are the cards' positions, not a claim that its Introductory learner is at L3.
