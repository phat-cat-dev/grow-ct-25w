# How *Excelling at University* plugs into Reflective Practice

The core is generic and cites the guide only as the source of the model's claims; this map says where the guide teaches each part of the pathway (N-0012). Pages and section names are the guide's; descriptions are ing's paraphrase, never extracts of more than 25 words. Capability ids are the model's, or ing's temporary ones (`/cN`). Where the guide has no content for a capability, the row says so.

**Chapters:** Chapter 4 (Becoming a Self-Regulating Learner), its section on reflective practice (pp. 53-55); the reflective writing genre in Chapter 9 (pp. 161-163); Kolb's cycle in Chapter 12 (pp. 225-228).

## 1. The guide's sections for this pathway

| Pages | What the guide teaches there |
|---|---|
| pp. 53-55 | Reflective practice: reflection as the evaluation phase of self-regulated learning; a describe, reflect, take action cycle (after Driscoll and Jasper); reflective journals as assessed tasks; reflection at the heart of continuing professional development and lifelong learning |
| pp. 55-58 | Becoming a self-regulating learner: monitoring and amending strategies that are not working |
| pp. 161-163 | Reflective writing as a genre, worked through the ERA cycle (Jasper, 2013) with a student's reflection |
| pp. 225-228 | Kolb's experiential learning cycle: experiencing, reflecting, thinking, acting |
| p. 64 | A student uses reflection on formative feedback to create an action plan |

## 3. What the guide does not cover

Reflexivity, Johns's model and Gibbs's stages are not taught in the guide; Moon's levels (the A11 scale) are not in the guide. The professional-practice reflective report (D-ing-0044) is not in the guide: its reflective writing (pp. 161-163) is in essay form; the report's headings come from Gibbs (core, Part E item 12), and the ERA cycle maps onto them.
