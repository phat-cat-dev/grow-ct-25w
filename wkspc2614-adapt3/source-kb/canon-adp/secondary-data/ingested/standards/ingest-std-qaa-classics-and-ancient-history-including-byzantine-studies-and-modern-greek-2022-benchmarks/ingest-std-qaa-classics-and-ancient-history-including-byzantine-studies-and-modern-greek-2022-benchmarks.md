### 0.1 Agent definition

This document records what the QAA's 2022 statement for Classics and Ancient History, with Byzantine Studies and Modern Greek, expects of an honours graduate and of the courses that lead there. Read S4 for the standards: section 4 gives three rungs, threshold (third class), typical (2:1 or 2:2) and excellent (first class), each a list of examples of evidence, and section 1 adds four lists of the knowledge, qualities of mind and abilities a graduate has. ### 0.2 Summary
<!-- label: both; status: drafted; basis: primary -->
Classics and Ancient History (including Byzantine Studies and Modern Greek), bachelor's degree with honours, MA (Hons) in Scotland (FHEQ level 6; FQHEIS level 10), fifth edition, March 2022. Three rungs are stated, each led by a paragraph naming the class: threshold (4.3, 10 items), typical (4.4, 11 items) and excellent (4.5, 12 items); the lists run in near parallel, so most outcomes are traced across all three. Section 1 lists the subject-specific characteristics of graduates (1.13, 7 items), the cognitive attributes (1.14, 7), the specific abilities (1.15, 8) and the practical capacities (1.16, 7). Fourteen paragraphs oblige or state outcomes: facing the subject's history of elitism, inclusive and accessible teaching, progression and joint honours, sensitive material, language requirements of Classics and Modern Greek courses, language pathways, adjustments for field and museum work, assessment and Annex D. Themes: equality, diversity and inclusion; accessibility; sustainability (education for sustainable development); entrepreneurship and enterprise education with employability. No professional, statutory or regulatory body accredits the subject; subject associations and the British Schools at Athens and Rome are named as partners.

### SB1 The subject
<!-- label: both; status: drafted; basis: primary -->
- The study of ancient Greece and Rome from the late Bronze Age to Late Antiquity, of Byzantium from 324 to 1453 CE and of the Greek-speaking world from the late medieval period on, widening to other ancient cultures and to reception in every period (D1, 1.1, 3.1, 3.2, 3.3).
- Interdisciplinary by nature: art, archaeology, history, language and linguistics, literature, philology, philosophy, law, science, medicine and reception, studied in varying mixes (D1, 1.1, 1.20).
- Several routes into the classical: Classics with both ancient languages, single-language Greek or Latin, Classical Studies, Ancient History, Classical Archaeology, Modern Greek and Late Antique and Byzantine Studies (D1, 1.2, 3.5 to 3.12).
- Languages are central but not universal: Classics and Modern Greek require proficiency, other routes offer languages at several entry points (D1, 2.1, 3.6, 3.9, 3.12, 3.20).
- Critical handling of fragmentary, varied evidence, material, written and digital, is its characteristic method (D1, 1.20, 1.13).
- It sees Greek and Roman societies as linked with each other and with the wider Mediterranean and beyond, through influence, conflict and borrowing (D1, 1.21, 1.24).
- A self-critical discipline: it confronts its reputation for elitism and its ties to empire, colonialism, race and class (D1, 1.4, 1.22 to 1.24).
- A liberal, non-vocational tradition that nonetheless builds a broad platform of transferable skills valued by employers (D1, 1.5, 1.6, 1.17, 1.31).
