## Part 0 Summary

### 0.1 Agent definition
<!-- label: editorial; status: drafted; basis: primary -->
QAA's UK reference point for what an honours graduate in Politics and International Relations should know, understand and be able to do at the point of award. It gives one rung only, the threshold, in three lists (knowledge and understanding; discipline-specific skills; cognitive abilities and skills) and leaves performance above the threshold to Annex D of QAA's Frameworks. Reach for it when designing, reviewing or describing a politics, international relations or related course, its outcomes, its assessment or its treatment of the four cross-cutting themes. It is not a curriculum and not a regulatory requirement, and it sets no master's standards.

### 0.2 Summary
<!-- label: both; status: drafted; basis: primary -->
Published by QAA in March 2023 as the fifth edition, the statement describes the discipline (power, governance, conflict and cooperation, at every scale from local to global), its purposes and distinctive features and the content, teaching and assessment of an honours degree. It treats four cross-cutting themes: equality, diversity and inclusion; accessibility; sustainability (education for sustainable development, with the SDGs); and enterprise and entrepreneurship education. Its benchmark standards are threshold only: 28 items in three lists at 4.3 to 4.5, for the bachelor's degree with honours at FHEQ level 6 and FQHEIS level 10, with Annex D cited for the classes above the threshold. A further skills list (3.3) names abilities a course may develop. It names no professional, statutory or regulatory body for the subject; master's study at FHEQ level 7 is mentioned only as resting on the same principles at a higher level.

### SB1 The subject
<!-- label: both; status: drafted; basis: primary -->
- The field studies power, governance, participation, decision-making, conflict and cooperation at every scale, from local to global (D1, 1.1, 1.3).
- Politics asks who gets what, when, how, why and where, around questions of power, justice, legitimacy, sovereignty and security; international relations adds a world split into many polities, states and the actors above, below and beyond them (D1, 1.8, 1.9).
- Its ways of knowing combine description, general explanation, normative theory and political philosophy, positive political theory and approaches that reject universal theories, such as historical and postmodern analysis (D1, 1.7).
- It uses a wide range of quantitative and qualitative methods and draws on cognate subjects (D1, 1.10).
- Its boundaries and names are contested and shifting, and it treats its own history (imperial, colonial, patriarchal and other) as an object of critical study (D1, 1.3, 1.6, 1.11, 1.12).
- It prepares graduates for careers, active citizenship and leading change, in policy, enterprise, public institutions and beyond (D1, 1.1, 1.22).
