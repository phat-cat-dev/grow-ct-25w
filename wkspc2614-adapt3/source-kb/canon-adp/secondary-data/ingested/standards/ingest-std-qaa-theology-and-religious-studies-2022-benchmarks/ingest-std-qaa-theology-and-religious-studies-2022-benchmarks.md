## Part 0 Summary

### 0.1 Agent definition
<!-- label: editorial; status: drafted; basis: primary -->
QAA's UK reference point for what an honours graduate in theology and religious studies can be expected to know, understand and do, set as threshold (third-class) outcomes in three lists: knowledge and understanding, subject-specific and intellectual skills and generic skills; an appendix adds what a taught master's in the field shows on top of them. Use it when designing, reviewing or describing a theology, religious studies or divinity course and its outcomes. It is not a curriculum, not a checklist and not a regulatory requirement in England; it states no typical or excellent lists: above the threshold it hands over to the degree classification descriptions (Annex D).

### 0.2 Summary
<!-- label: both; status: drafted; basis: primary -->
Published by QAA in March 2022 as the fifth edition, the statement covers theology and religious studies (TRS) as one contested field taught in secular and faith-based settings, under many course titles. It describes the subject's context and purposes, its distinctive features (breadth and depth designs, access, progression, partnership with religious communities, review), its content, approaches, teaching and assessment; and benchmark standards at the threshold rung only, for bachelor's degrees with honours (FHEQ level 6, FQHEIS level 10): 24 outcomes. Appendix A adds eight outcomes for taught master's degrees. It carries the cross-cutting themes of equality, diversity and inclusion (with a decolonising approach), accessibility, education for sustainable development (with the eight UNESCO competencies) and employability with enterprise and entrepreneurship education. It points to Annex D for performance above the threshold and names PSRBs in general, faith bodies, the Institute for Apprenticeships and Technical Education (a Church Minister standard) and the subject association's Framework of Professional Practice.

### SB1 The subject
<!-- label: both; status: drafted; basis: primary -->
- A field that studies religion and religions critically and globally, including non-religion, atheism and the secular, so that people can understand and act in a world religion shapes (D1, 1.1, 1.11, 3.1, 3.2).
- Its identity is itself debated: one internally varied discipline, or theology and religious studies as two linked disciplines; insider and outsider stances and views on truth claims cut across the debate (D1, 1.3, 1.4).
- It is taught in secular providers open to any belief and in providers with a religious stance, some training for ministry or chaplaincy, under many course titles (D1, 1.5 to 1.8, 2.6).
- Courses are built for breadth (several traditions) or depth (one tradition, often with its languages) or a mix (D1, 2.1, 2.8, 3.4).
- It works through many approaches, textual, historical, social scientific, philosophical, theological, feminist, postcolonial and others, and draws on and feeds many neighbouring subjects (D1, 3.6, 3.7, 4.7).
- It engages directly with living religion through study visits, fieldwork, placements and partnership with religious communities, which calls for respect and handles unsettling critique (D1, 2.19, 2.20, 3.12, 3.13).
- It can change students personally, but a student's spiritual development and religious outlook are not award criteria (D1, 1.12, 4.5, A5).
