About this table

Fourteen academic practices, nine cards each, read from foundation to level 8: from basic procedure towards the metacognitive and the reflexive. Rows are card positions. Each row is read three ways, as practice (what one does), knowledge (what one knows) and practitioner self-identity (who one is). Each academic practice has six columns: the card, the card read as practice, knowledge and practitioner self-identity, its kind of knowledge and the FHEQ or CQFW statements the card cites. Under each reading come the FHEQ descriptors that answer it at the row's level: what each looks like in the student's disciplinary, professional and personal development in this practice, and how it is evidenced across the disciplines (FHEQ descriptor plus the practice's own practice and knowledge gives the practitioner's practice, knowledge and self-identity). Conceptual models come next, each placed against the rows. Of development: Barnett's levels of critical being in knowledge, self and world, Biggs and Collis's SOLO taxonomy, Dreyfus's stages of skill, Baxter Magolda's journey to self-authorship, Lave and Wenger's participation in a community of practice, loosely the student life cycle, Shulman's signature pedagogies (to think, to perform, to act with integrity), Kuhlthau's information search process in feelings, thoughts and actions, Lea and Street's academic literacies and Finlay's variants of reflexivity, Meyer and Land's threshold concepts, self-determination theory and Mezirow's transformative learning; the life cycle now names Gale and Parker's and Lizzio's models of transition. Of knowledge and metacognition: Anderson and Krathwohl, Marzano and Kendall, Zimmerman, Schraw and Dennison, the Education Endowment Foundation, Efklides and Kuhn. Professional frameworks follow, each across every row: social work's Professional Capabilities Framework, Benner's stages from novice to expert in nursing and the NHS career framework of Skills for Health. The qualification frameworks follow, placed by each row's indicative level: FHEQ, CQFW, the Scottish framework (FQHEIS, within SCQF levels 7 to 12) and the Bologna framework for the European Higher Education Area (its Dublin descriptors, short cycle to third cycle). Cross-cutting themes follow, comparing academic practice with wider professional practice: enterprise and entrepreneurship, employability, education for sustainable development and the occupational competence levels used for apprenticeships. Then each of 54 subject benchmarks, read the same three ways where the benchmark speaks: level 5 at d2, level 6 at d3 (threshold) and e2 (typical and excellent), level 7 at e3. Hover a header for the subject in a sentence, or a cell for the statement ids it rests on.

Foundation and level 8 rows come from each practice's progression by level (levels 3 and 8); Self-Development's are from its new core, version 0.1.0. Row readings (practice, knowledge, practitioner self-identity, stance) and the placing of each model and theme against a row are working interpretations, not rulings; Barnett, SOLO, Lave and Wenger, Shulman, Kuhlthau, Lea and Street, Finlay, Anderson and Krathwohl and Marzano and Kendall are paraphrased and checked against held summaries of the texts; the other models are paraphrased from ing's knowledge and unverified until their texts are held (hover a model's name for its reference). Framework and theme cells give the issuer's id and a paraphrase; issuers' wording is not reproduced. The apprenticeship column follows each row's indicative level (Ofqual's occupational competence descriptors; levels 6 and 7 are the degree apprenticeship levels).
