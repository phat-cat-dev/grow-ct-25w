3 · Teaching cycles — a loop with continuous flow

When to use a cycle

  • The order repeats. Water cycle, feedback loops, the design process — anything with no real end.
  • Animate the flow, not each node. A single dot travelling the loop says "continuous" better than six separate effects.
  • Draw the arcs in sequence on first view, then let the runner loop. Stop both under reduced motion.
  • Pair with blocks: one Adapt block per stage, revealed by Trickle as the learner completes each.
4 · Timelines — a line that draws, milestones that arrive
Step 01

Observe

Gather what you notice before explaining it.

Step 02

Interpret

Offer a reading, and say what it rests on.

Step 03

Test

Check the reading against one more case.

Step 04

Revise

Keep, change or drop it — and note why.

When to use a timeline

  • The order is the point. A real sequence, process or history where "before/after" carries meaning.
  • Draw the spine first, then let each milestone rise in turn — the eye follows the line down.
  • Tie reveals to progress: in a course, map each milestone to a block and let Adapt's onscreen hook or Trickle trigger the rise.
  • Keep it readable stopped. At rest the whole timeline is visible; the animation only re-tells the order on request.
