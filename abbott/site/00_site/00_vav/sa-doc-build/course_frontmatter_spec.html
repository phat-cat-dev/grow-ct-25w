Course frontmatter

  1. 01

    Orientation

    Defined per course The course is the Level 0 node

    Understood: the course is the Level 0 node. It is one coherent concept, broken down into lessons. It is also a node in its own right, so the record has to work as a document and as a concept node that other nodes can point at.

    This is the plan for the course record. It extends the Level 0 section of frontmatter_spec.md and keeps its field names where they exist. Nothing here is an enforced schema. It is a proposed convention.

  2. 02

    What changes between a lesson and a course

    Defined per course Four extra jobs

    A lesson record says what one lesson teaches. A course record has four extra jobs.

    Name the concept
    State the one idea the course is about, define it and say what binds the lessons together.
    Place the concept
    Say where the concept sits among other concepts: what it belongs to, what it needs first, what it is easily confused with.
    Decompose it
    Map every lesson to the concept: its role, the terms it introduces and the outcomes it serves.
    Open the course
    Give the learner a front page and a lesson menu without anyone retyping the data.

    Because the course is a node, every field that describes it should be readable on its own, without the document body. A viewer, a graph or a search index should be able to read the front matter and draw the node.

  3. 03

    Four layers

    Kept internal Keep these apart, as the lesson spec does
    1 · Document metadata
    Identity, hierarchy, ownership, version, review and rights.
    2 · Concept node
    The concept, its definition, terms, relations and coherence claim.
    3 · Learning design
    Audience, scope, outcomes, lesson map, delivery and assessment.
    4 · Learner-visible front matter
    The course opening and the lesson menu.

    Layers 1 to 3 are the authoritative record. Layer 4 is a display view of them. The course manifest stays the source of truth for design decisions, so this record is an authoring view of the manifest, not a competing copy.

    For a course, document_type is course-scope.

  6. 06

    The concept node block

    Defined per course The part a lesson record does not need

    This is the part a lesson record does not need.

    concept.node_id
    Stable identifier for the concept. It does not change when the label changes. It is separate from the document id, so one concept can outlive a rewrite of the course.
    concept.label, alt_labels
    Preferred name and the other names people use for it.
    concept.definition
    A short definition that stands without the body.
    concept.coherence_claim
    The reason the lessons form one course. If you cannot write it, the course may be two courses.
    concept.through_line
    The question that every lesson helps answer. Each lesson summary should connect to it.
    concept.key_terms
    Terms the course defines, each with the lesson that introduces it. Learners meet a term first in one place.
    concept.threshold_concepts
    Ideas that change how learners see the whole topic. Leave empty if there are none. Do not invent them.
    concept.misconceptions
    Common wrong ideas, each tied to the lesson that addresses it.
    concept.relations
    Typed links to other concept nodes (see below).
    concept.external_refs
    Identifiers or links in outside vocabularies. Use only confirmed ones.

    Relation predicates

    Use a small controlled set so the links can be drawn and queried:

    broader
    This concept sits inside the target concept.
    narrower
    The target concept sits inside this one.
    requires
    Learners need the target concept first.
    related
    Linked, with no ordering or containment.
    contrasts_with
    Easily confused with the target. Say how in note.
    applies
    This concept puts the target concept to use.

    State each link once, from the node that owns it. Do not record both directions by hand. A viewer can derive the reverse.

    Distinctions

    • concept.definition says what the idea is. scope says how much of it this course teaches.
    • coherence_claim explains why the lessons belong together. lesson_map lists them.
    • concept.relations link concepts. audience.prerequisites link courses and knowledge. A course can require a concept without requiring another course.
  7. 07

    The lesson map as the edges

    Derived Each entry joins the course node to a lesson node

    Each entry joins the course node to a lesson node, so give it enough to draw the join.

    lesson_id, sequence, title
    Which lesson, in what presentation order. Sequence is order, not depth.
    role
    How the lesson serves the concept: introduces, develops, applies, synthesises or assesses.
    course_outcome_ids
    Course outcomes the lesson serves.
    concept_term_ids
    Key terms the lesson teaches.
    requires_lesson_ids
    Lessons that must come first. This is dependency, which can differ from sequence.
    estimated_duration_minutes
    Lesson estimate, used to derive the course estimate.
    status
    Lesson lifecycle value, so the course view can show what is ready.

    Keep the lesson's own record authoritative for its content. The map holds only what the course needs to draw and check the whole.

    Lessons, as a menu

    Assessment detail and support information sit immediately below the menu, not in the header.

    Do not promise that a lesson is optional, saves answers, unlocks the next lesson or affects completion unless that behaviour has been verified. Recording an intention in lesson_order or status does not configure Adapt or SCORM.

  9. 09

    Inheritance rules

    Inherited Inherit, define, derive, keep internal, keep runtime separate
    Inherit from the course
    The fields listed in inherits_to_lessons. A lesson may override one, and the override is visible in its own record.
    Define per course
    Concept, scope, outcomes, lesson map, assessment strategy and support information.
    Derive automatically
    Course duration from the lesson estimates, lesson position, the outcome-coverage table, breadcrumbs and the menu itself.
    Keep internal
    Authorship, approval records, versioning, scope exclusions, assumptions, misconceptions that are not shown to learners and implementation identifiers.
    Keep runtime separate
    Adapt IDs, tracking IDs, assessment configuration and completion rules.
  10. 10

    Coherence checks

    Kept internal The record can be checked mechanically

    Because the course is one concept, the record can be checked mechanically. A check that fails is a defect in the plan, not a style note.

    1. Every course outcome is served by at least one lesson.
    2. Every lesson serves at least one course outcome.
    3. Every key term is introduced in exactly one lesson, and that lesson exists in the map.
    4. Every misconception is addressed in at least one existing lesson.
    5. requires_lesson_ids form no cycle, and no lesson requires a later lesson unless lesson_order allows free order.
    6. sequence values are unique and run without gaps.
    7. Nothing appears in both in_scope and out_of_scope.
    8. Every concept.relations target resolves to a known node, or is flagged as external.
    9. The course duration equals the sum of its lesson estimates, or the record says why not.
    10. Learner-facing duration, level and outcomes match the authoritative fields, or are derived from them.
  11. 11

    Decisions to settle before building the menu page

    Kept internal Four open questions
    1. Concept id. One concept.node_id per course, or can a course carry more than one concept node? This plan assumes one.
    2. Relation vocabulary. The six predicates above are a starting set. Keep them, or align them to a vocabulary you already use.
    3. Lesson roles. Five roles are proposed. Add or drop to match how you plan courses.
    4. Free or guided order. delivery.lesson_order decides whether the menu can offer any lesson at any time. Confirm what Adapt can enforce before the page implies either.

    Bottom line

    The course record defines the concept, its place among other concepts and how the lessons break it down. Each lesson record defines its contribution to that concept. The learner-facing front matter and the menu are views of that record, derived from it and never retyped.
